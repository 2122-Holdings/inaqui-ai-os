# How I Work With AI

I don't use AI as a smarter search box or as a collection of isolated prompts. I use it as part of an operating system for getting work done.

My starting point is rarely "what can AI do here?" It is: what are we trying to accomplish, what do we know, where does the truth live, what remains uncertain, and what needs to happen next?

I came to this as an operator. For more than a decade my job has been to build, scale and fix businesses. AI became useful to me when it stopped being another tool I had to manage and started helping me connect strategy, information and execution.

The biggest change for me has been span of control. AI lets me go deeper into more workstreams at the same time — commercial, operational, analytical or technical — without treating each one as an isolated conversation.

That does not come from better prompting alone. It comes from persistent project context: the objective, operating rules, decisions, open questions, current state and canonical sources. With that foundation, I can change models or tools without asking the project to start over.

## Start with the objective

The roadmap, business objective or engineering goal provides direction. I choose the environment according to the work: ChatGPT or Claude/Cowork for some connected research and operating workflows, Codex for repository-based engineering, and specialist systems for work such as GIS or meeting capture.

These environments do not have identical capabilities. What is interchangeable is their role in continuity. No LLM is the system of record, so replacing one execution environment should not erase the project's memory.

## Give AI context, not just prompts

A good prompt can improve one answer. Persistent context improves the work that follows.

Before meaningful work begins, I want the AI environment to understand the project scope, what has been decided, which sources are authoritative, what remains open and what it is allowed to do. The model enters the project, understands its current position and continues from there.

This is what expands my span of control: not having more conversations, but maintaining depth and continuity across more areas of work.

## Turn knowledge into execution

Meetings, research and analysis should not end as summaries. They should change a decision, create a task, close a question, update the roadmap or become durable knowledge.

The operating loop is straightforward:

```text
Operating Roadmap
→ Questions
→ Meetings / Research / Data
→ Structured Knowledge
→ Decisions & Tasks
→ Execution
→ Follow-up
→ Validation
→ Updated Context
↺
```

Open questions tell the system what we still need to learn. Meetings, research and data generate evidence. Evidence moves decisions. Decisions create execution. Follow-up prevents work from disappearing. Validation tells us whether it actually worked.

I keep one distinction explicit:

```text
Evidence → Recommendation → Decision → Authorized Action → Validation
```

Research can support a recommendation without making the decision. AI can help prepare and execute work without inheriting the authority or accountability behind it.

## Close the loop

A checked task is not necessarily a completed outcome. When the result matters, I look for evidence that the expected change happened — by checking the source system, testing a workflow, reviewing an artifact or validating an engineering change.

When reality materially changes, I update the project's durable context. The next work cycle then starts from an accurate position rather than from a meeting, inbox or conversation history.

## Make capabilities compound

When a workflow consistently produces value, I preserve the method behind it. Meeting Intelligence, Evidence-Based Research, Geospatial Intelligence, Artifact Generation, Decision & Task Orchestration, Follow-up, and Engineering Execution & Verification can become reusable capabilities rather than one-off outputs.

The reusable part is not the private data or a particular tool. It is the operating method: the inputs it needs, the sequence of work, the output it produces and how that output is validated.

POW Water is one example. The project can close or pause while methods developed around GIS, research, meeting intelligence or execution loops survive and inform future work.

A project can end without its capabilities disappearing.

PhotoVault applies the same logic in a different environment. Persistent repository context, architectural decisions, experiments, testing and verification make engineering work cumulative even when the AI environment changes.

## Before and after

Before, work often ended where it was produced:

```text
Meeting → Notes
Research → Document
Analysis → Presentation
Task → Checklist
Project → Conversation history
```

Now those inputs can contribute to one persistent loop:

```text
Meeting / Research / Data / Files
→ Evidence
→ Decisions
→ Execution
→ Validation
→ Updated Context
↺
```

The improvement is not that AI does everything. It is that previously disconnected work can contribute to the same execution system.

## What I do not automate

I do not automate simply because something can be automated. I prefer simple and manual first, until repetition, value and failure modes justify building more.

Automation should not hide uncertainty. If a source is unclear, evidence conflicts or an important assumption remains unresolved, sometimes the correct output is a question rather than an answer.

## The goal

The goal is not to automate my job. It is to increase the amount and complexity of work I can understand, coordinate and execute without losing context, judgment or accountability.

Models can change. Project memory should survive. Capabilities should compound.
